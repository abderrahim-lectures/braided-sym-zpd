# Audit report — member m4, direction d8: the uncited background layer

**Scope.** This report audits the assertions of `paper/braided-sym-zpd.tex` that carry **no
locator at all** (or only a section locator), and that are *not* one of the paper's own numbered
results: the mathematical background the paper states in passing and expects the reader (and the
referee) to accept. The numbered-statement fidelity checks against Brešar (Def 1.1, Prop 1.3,
Cor 2.17, Ex 1.10/2.19/2.23, Thm 2.27, Def 7.1, Thm 7.2), the §4/rem:inner mathematics and the
$M_2(\mathbb F_3)$ numerics, the Adrabi/Horn–Johnson text comparison and the hypothesis
stress-tests of the numbered statements are other members' directions and are **not** redone here;
where I touch the same sentence I say so. Line numbers are lines of the `.tex` file as delivered.

**Method.** `pdftotext -layout` per PDF page with explicit `===== PDFPAGE n =====` markers
(`.scratch/audit/m4/{bresar,adrabi,hj}_pages.txt`, built by `.scratch/audit/m4/pagetag.py`), so
every quotation below carries the **printed** page number, read off the running head of the page it
is on (the PDF/print offset is *not* constant: PDF page 12 is printed p. 4, PDF page 129 is printed
p. 126, PDF page 179 is printed p. 177 — so page numbers were always taken from the heads, never
computed). Numerical claims are checked by
`results/audit-scripts/m4-uncited-background.py` (numpy, exact arithmetic over $\mathbb F_2$).

## F1 — `rem:inner`, l.448 — the elements $(e_{ii}+be_{ij})-e_{ii}$ are **not** idempotents

**Paper text** (l.448–450): *"for a matrix algebra, $M_n(B)$ is generated by the idempotents
$(e_{ii} + be_{ij}) - e_{ii}$ for $i \ne j$ with $b \in B$"*.

**Verdict: ERROR.** In $M_n(B)$, $e_{ij}e_{ij}=0$ for $i\neq j$, hence
$(be_{ij})^2=0$, so $(e_{ii}+be_{ij})-e_{ii}=be_{ij}$ is **square-zero**; it is an idempotent only
when $b=0$. Computation: with $B=M_2(\mathbb F_2)$ (noncommutative, unital) and $n=3$, i.e. inside
$M_3(M_2(\mathbb F_2))\cong M_6(\mathbb F_2)$, for all $90$ cases $i\neq j$, $b\neq0$ the element
$be_{ij}$ is idempotent in **0** cases, while $e_{ii}+be_{ij}$ is idempotent in **96/96** cases
(`CHECK 1A`, `CHECK 1B` of the script).

**Source text** (Brešar 2021, proof of Corollary 2.4, **printed p. 19 = PDF p. 27**): *"If $i \neq j$, then
$e_{ii} + se_{ij}$ is an idempotent, so $se_{ij} = (e_{ii}+se_{ij}) - e_{ii}$ is a difference of two
idempotents. From $se_{ii} = se_{ij}\cdot e_{ji}$, where $j \neq i$, it thus follows that $se_{ii}$
also lies in the subring generated by idempotents."* (The `pdftotext -layout` extraction prints the
glyph $\neq$ as `=`: the passage comes out as "If i = j, then eii + seij is an idempotent … where
j = i", which is inconsistent with the surrounding computation $se_{ii}=se_{ij}e_{ji}$; the
inequality is therefore certain even though the extracted glyph is not.)

**Minimal repair.** *"$M_n(B)$ with $n\ge 2$ and $B$ unital is generated by the idempotents
$e_{ii}+be_{ij}$ ($i\neq j$, $b\in B$), because $be_{ij}=(e_{ii}+be_{ij})-e_{ii}$ is a difference of
two idempotents."* Two clauses are wrong as printed: the elements named are not idempotents, and
$n\ge2$ is missing (for $n=1$ the claim is false: $B$ need not be generated by idempotents).
With the repair the generation holds — the corrected set generates all of $M_3(M_2(\mathbb F_2))$
(dimension $36/36$, `CHECK 1C`). *(This is one of the sentences assigned to the §4/rem:inner
direction; the contribution here is the source quotation fixing the exact repair.)*

## F2 — `rem:inner`, l.449–450 — dropped hypothesis "unital"; the theorem number is unverifiable

**Paper text:** *"an algebra generated by idempotents is zJpd in characteristic other than $2$
\citep[Theorem 2.1]{AnLiHe2015}"*.

**Source text available in the folder** (Brešar 2021, Theorem 3.15, **printed p. 49 = PDF p. 57**): *"Let $A$ be
an (associative) **unital** algebra over a field $F$ of characteristic not $2$. If $A$ is generated
by idempotents, then $A$ is zJpd."* Brešar writes immediately before it (p. 49): *"The following theorem was
obtained in [24] (and partially in [85])"*, and his entry **[24]** (References, **p. 176 = PDF p. 178**) is
*"G. An, J. Li, J. He, Zero Jordan product determined algebras. Linear Algebra Appl. 475, 90–93
(2015)"* — i.e. exactly the paper's `AnLiHe2015`.

**Verdict: IMPRECISE.** The mathematical content is corroborated (and its bibliographic data match
Brešar's entry exactly), but (i) the paper's paraphrase drops "unital" and "associative", which the
version available in the folder requires, and (ii) *Theorem 2.1* of An–Li–He cannot be checked: that
paper is not in the folder, so neither its numbering nor its hypotheses (in particular whether it
assumes a unity) can be verified. **Repair:** write *"a unital algebra generated by idempotents
…"* and add `\citep[Theorem 3.15]{Bresar2021}` next to the An–Li–He citation.

## F3 — Introduction, l.116 — "studied since Banach and Lamperti" (§7.1)

**Paper text:** *"Such maps have been studied since Banach and Lamperti \citep[\S7.1]{Bresar2021}."*

**Source text** (Brešar 2021, §7.1, **printed p. 114 = PDF p. 117**): *"The study of zero product preserving
maps has a long history. We can start by mentioning that in his classical book [28], Banach studied
the form of isometries of $L_p([0,1])$ and made the crucial observation that they send functions
with disjoint support into functions with disjoint support. His work was later completed by
Lamperti [117]."*

**Verdict: FINE.** The locator is right and the sentence is a fair compression of Brešar's two
sentences. (A referee might prefer "the isometry classification of Banach and Lamperti" since what
Banach and Lamperti actually classified is isometries of $L_p$; this is style, not defect.)

## F6 — §2, l.178 and l.222 — the convention $A^+$ and the identification with zJpd

**Paper text** (l.178): *"Here $A^+$ has the Jordan product $x \circ y = xy + yx$ in place of $xy$."*
(l.222): *"for $\mu = \circ$ it is the zJpd property of Definition~\ref{def:bresar-zjpd}"*.

**Source text** (Brešar 2021, §3.1, **printed p. 36 = PDF p. 44**): *"Every associative algebra $A$ becomes a
Jordan algebra, denoted $A^+$, if we replace the original product by the Jordan product
$x\circ y = xy+yx$."* (Def. 3.14, **p. 48 = PDF p. 56**: *"An associative algebra $A$ is zero Jordan product
determined if the Jordan algebra $A^+$ is zero product determined."*)

**Verdict: FINE.** The paper uses the factor-free Jordan product (no $\tfrac12$), matching Brešar, so
"determined by $\circ$" $=$ "$A^+$ is zpd" $=$ zJpd. No hypothesis is missing: both notions are
char-free.

## F7 — §4, l.309 — the characteristic-2 clause

**Paper text:** *"In characteristic $2$ the map $\mu$ is zero, every $\varphi$ in
Definition~\ref{def:determined} is zero, and every algebra is determined by $\mu$."*

**Verdict: FINE**, and the reasoning is complete as it stands: if $\mu\equiv0$ the hypothesis
"$\varphi$ vanishes whenever $\mu(x,y)=0$" is vacuous, so $\varphi\equiv0$, and $\psi=0$ factors it.
Checked on $A=M_2(\mathbb F_2)$: $\mu(x,y)=2xy=0$ for $256/256$ pairs (`CHECK 3`).

## F8 — §4, l.311–313 — "recovers the usual form of the zJpd criterion"

**Paper text:** *"When $\operatorname{char} K \neq 2$ the linear map $\overline{\circ} \colon
A \otimes A \to A$ with $x \otimes y \mapsto x \circ y$ is onto, because $x \circ \tfrac12 = x$. So
Corollary~\ref{cor:criterion} recovers the usual form of the zJpd criterion."*

**Source check.** Brešar's §3.3 (**printed pp. 48–60**, chapter 4 begins on p. 61) contains Def. 3.14, Thm. 3.15,
Rem. 3.16, Cor. 3.17, Exx. 3.18–3.22, Def. 3.21, Thm. 3.23, Rem. 3.24, Cor. 3.25, Exx. 3.26–3.28:
there is **no separately numbered "zJpd criterion"** in the form the paper refers to. The criterion the paper means is
Prop. 1.3(iii) (**printed p. 4**) applied to $A^+$: *"(iii) If $\sum_i z_iw_i=0$, then
$\sum_i z_i\otimes w_i$ … lies in $\operatorname{span}\{x\otimes y \in A\otimes A \mid xy=0\}$"* —
and that form needs **no surjectivity**.

**Verdict: IMPRECISE** (substance right, reference and grammar loose). Two defects: (i) "the usual
form of the zJpd criterion" has no locatable referent — the kernel criterion is Prop. 1.3(iii) for
the algebra $A^+$, and the surjectivity of $\overline{\circ}$ is not needed for it (it is needed only
for the "$\varphi(x,y)=\varphi(x\circ y,1)$" form, Brešar's Cor. 1.5, p. 6); (ii) the markup
"$x\circ\tfrac12$" multiplies an element by a scalar inside $\circ$; it should be $x\circ(\tfrac12 1)$.
**Repair:** *"… because $x\circ(\tfrac12 1)=x$. So Corollary~\ref{cor:criterion}, applied to $A^+$
(Definition~\ref{def:bresar-zjpd}), is the zJpd criterion."*

## F9 — §4, l.314 — the "indeed" parenthetical for the Jordan product

**Paper text:** *"It is not of the form $f(xy)$ on a noncommutative algebra (indeed $x = 1$ forces
$f = 2\,\mathrm{id}$: if $\operatorname{char} K \neq 2$ then $xy + yx = 2xy$ gives $yx = xy$, while if
$\operatorname{char} K = 2$ then $f = 0$, so $xy + yx = 0$ for all $x, y$, which fails as soon as
$xy \neq yx$)"*.

**Verdict: FINE.** Both branches check: $x=1$ gives $f(y)=y+y=2y$, so $f=2\,\mathrm{id}$; in
char $\neq 2$ this forces $yx=xy$; in char $2$, $f=2\,\mathrm{id}=0$ forces $xy+yx=0$, and in char $2$
$xy+yx=0$ is equivalent to $xy=yx$, so it does fail as soon as $xy\neq yx$.

## F10 — §4, l.370–371 — "id + α is twice the projection"

**Paper text:** *"When $\alpha$ is an involution and $\operatorname{char} K \neq 2$, the map
$\mathrm{id} + \alpha$ is twice the projection onto the fixed points of $\alpha$. It is injective
only for $\alpha = \mathrm{id}$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:injective} gives no converse."*

**Verdict: FINE.** $P=\tfrac12(\mathrm{id}+\alpha)$ satisfies $P^2 = \tfrac14(\mathrm{id}+2\alpha+\alpha^2)=P$
(here char $\neq2$ and $\alpha^2=\mathrm{id}$), and $P$ projects onto $\operatorname{Fix}\alpha$; a
projection $P\neq\mathrm{id}$ has $0\neq\ker P$, so $\mathrm{id}+\alpha=2P$ is injective iff
$P=\mathrm{id}$ iff $\alpha=\mathrm{id}$.

## F11 — §4, l.395 — "N is a two-sided ideal of codimension 1 that does not contain 1"

**Paper text:** *"In words, the hypotheses say that $N$ is a two-sided ideal of codimension $1$ that
does not contain $1$."*

**Verdict: FINE.** "$bn,nb\in N$ for $n\in N$, $b\in A$" is exactly that $N$ is a two-sided ideal;
"$f(1)\neq0$" is $1\notin N=\ker f$; and "$x-c(x)1\in N$ for a linear functional $c$" is
$A=N\oplus K1$, i.e. $\operatorname{codim}N=1$ (conversely, such an $N$ gives $c$ as the projection).
The equivalence is exact, not merely one-directional.

## F12 — `ex:dual`, l.407–412 — the dual numbers are $(K\varepsilon)^{\#}$; their only idempotents are $0,1$

**Paper text:** *"$A = K[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$, an algebra of the form studied in
\citep[Example 2.23]{Bresar2021}, where a unity is adjoined to an algebra with trivial
multiplication; here that algebra is $K\varepsilon$. … The only idempotents of $A$ are $0$ and $1$, so
$A$ is not generated by idempotents."*

**Source text** (Brešar 2021, Example 2.23, **printed p. 26 = PDF p. 34**): *"… let us look more closely at the
simplest case where $A$ has trivial multiplication. Then $A^\#$, the algebra obtained by adjoining a
unity to $A$, is a commutative unital algebra. … Thus, $\varphi$ is of the desired form, meaning that
$A^\#$ is indeed an example of a symmetrically zpd algebra that is not zpd."*

**Computation** (`CHECK 2`): for $K=\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5,\mathbb F_7$ the idempotents
of $K[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ are exactly $\{0,1\}$ and the subalgebra they generate is
$K\cdot 1$ ($2/4$, $3/9$, $5/25$, $7/49$ elements), i.e. $A$ is not generated by idempotents.
(Algebraically: $(a+b\varepsilon)^2=a^2+2ab\varepsilon=a+b\varepsilon$ gives $a^2=a$, $b(2a-1)=0$,
so $b=0$; the identification $(K\varepsilon)^\#\cong K[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ is
$(b\varepsilon,\lambda)\mapsto \lambda+b\varepsilon$.)

**Verdict: FINE.** Both the citation of Example 2.23 (construction, not conclusion) and the two
assertions are correct; the char-$\neq2$ hypothesis used by the surrounding example is not needed for
this particular claim.

## F13 — §1, l.135 — the three symmetrized products

**Paper text:** *"Their symmetrized products are $2xy$, $xy + yx$ and $xy + \alpha(y)\alpha(x)$."*

**Verdict: FINE.** With $x\cdot_\sigma y=m(x\otimes y+\sigma(x\otimes y))$ (l.130) and the three
braidings $\mathrm{id}$, $x\otimes y\mapsto y\otimes x$, $x\otimes y\mapsto \alpha(y)\otimes\alpha(x)$
(l.132–134): $m(x\otimes y+y\otimes x)=xy+yx$; $m(\alpha(y)\otimes\alpha(x))=\alpha(y)\alpha(x)$. No
commutativity or linearity beyond $K$-linearity of $\alpha$ is used.

## G2 — l.458 — "every automorphism of a matrix algebra is inner" has no support in the bibliography

**Paper text:** *"… the answer is affirmative, in characteristic other than $2$, whenever $\alpha$ is
inner, and every automorphism of a matrix algebra is inner; the question is therefore about algebras
admitting an involutive automorphism that is not inner."*

**Verdict: UNSUPPORTED.** The statement is classical (Skolem–Noether: $M_n(K)$ is central simple, so
every $K$-algebra automorphism is inner) and true for $M_n(K)$ with $K$ a field, $n\ge2$; but the
paper gives no locator, and the only matrix reference in its bibliography cannot supply one: a
case-insensitive search of the whole 664-page Horn–Johnson extraction for `automor`, `Skolem`,
`Noether` returns **0 hits** (for calibration, `matrix` returns 2850). It is also false as stated for
$M_n(B)$ with $B$ an arbitrary unital algebra, and the paper's own `rem:inner` works precisely in
$M_n(B)$: take $B=K\times K$ and $\sigma$ the swap of the two factors. $\sigma$ is not inner (every
unit of the commutative ring $K\times K$ acts trivially by conjugation), and the induced automorphism
$\operatorname{diag}(\sigma)$ of $M_n(K\times K)\cong M_n(K)\times M_n(K)$ sends $(X,Y)$ to $(Y,X)$,
which no componentwise conjugation $(X,Y)\mapsto(UXU^{-1},VYV^{-1})$ can do. **Repair:** *"every
automorphism of $M_n(K)$, $K$ a field, $n\ge2$, is inner (Skolem–Noether)"* with a citation.

## G3 — l.458 — "the answer is affirmative … whenever α is inner" overreads `rem:inner`

**Paper text:** as in G2.

**Verdict: IMPRECISE.** `rem:inner` establishes the affirmative answer only for $M_n(B)$ ($n\ge2$,
$B$ unital, characteristic $\ne2$), where the Jordan question is settled by zJpd of matrix algebras.
For a general noncommutative $A$ the inner case gives only the *equivalence* "determined by $\mu_\alpha$
$\iff$ determined by the Jordan product" (l.446), which is not an affirmative answer. **Repair:**
*"the answer is affirmative for $M_n(B)$, in characteristic other than $2$, whenever $\alpha$ is
inner; the question is therefore about algebras (other than matrix algebras over a field) admitting
an involutive automorphism that is not inner."*

## G4 — l.90, l.143 vs l.252 — the criterion is "our first result" and also Brice–Huang's Theorem 2.3

**Paper text.** Abstract l.90: *"The answer is a criterion in terms of the kernel of the induced map
$A \otimes A \to A$, and it needs no surjectivity hypothesis."* l.143: *"Our first result is a
criterion, which is Proposition 1.3 of Brešar for the algebra $(A,\mu)$."* l.252–253: *"The criterion
is stated for an arbitrary bilinear $\mu$, over a commutative ring, by Brice and Huang
\citep[Theorem 2.3]{BriceHuang2015}."*

**Verdict: IMPRECISE (attribution) / UNVERIFIABLE (the cited content).** `BriceHuang2015` is **not**
in the reference folder, so Theorem 2.3's statement, its commutative-ring generality and its number
cannot be checked; the only corroboration available is Brešar **p. 16 = PDF p. 24**: *"A more detailed analysis of
the topic of this section is given in [61]"*, with **[61]** (References, p. 177 = PDF p. 179) = *"D. Brice,
H. Huang, On zero product determined algebras. Linear Multilinear Algebra 63, 326–342 (2015)"* — which
supports the topic, not the theorem. If l.252 is accurate, then `cor:criterion` is the field case of a
published theorem, and calling it "our first result" in the Introduction while the abstract presents it
as the paper's answer is an over-claim a referee would catch. **Repair:** credit Brice–Huang
Theorem 2.3 in the abstract and in l.143 (e.g. "our first result is the field case of Brice–Huang's
criterion, in the form of Brešar's Proposition 1.3 for $(A,\mu)$"), or state precisely which
strengthening is new. What *is* verifiable locally: the criterion itself (Cor. `cor:criterion`) is
correct, since Prop. 1.3(i)$\Leftrightarrow$(iii) applied to $(A,\mu)$ says exactly
$\ker\overline\mu\subseteq\mathcal Z_\mu$.
